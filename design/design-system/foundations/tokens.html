The constraint that defines this system

These components are installed into other people's apps, which already have their own colors and typefaces. We cannot own color, and we must not impose a font. Therefore the identity has to live in form — geometry, proportion, spacing and motion. The bar: a JetCompose component should be recognisable in a greyscale screenshot. Everything below is chosen to survive being re-themed by a stranger.

1 — Shape: continuous curvature, not circular arcs

Every surface uses a superellipse ("squircle", n≈5) rather than a circular-arc border-radius. Circular arcs meet the straight edge abruptly — the curvature jumps from zero to maximum at one point, which the eye reads as slightly cheap. A superellipse eases curvature in continuously. It is the single most recognisable structural tell we have, it is visible with no colour at all, and Material does not use it.

2 — Colour: semantic roles, paired for both themes

Tokens are named by role, never by appearance, which is what lets a host app swap the whole system. Light is the default and the honest test — dark hides imprecise spacing and weak contrast. Both are designed, not derived. All pairs below meet WCAG AA for their use.

Note the accent is not the same hue value in both themes. A saturated blue that reads correctly on white is too heavy on near-black; the dark accent is lightened and desaturated so it carries equal weight. Reusing one value across themes is the most common design-system mistake.

3 — Spacing: 4pt grid with optical corrections

Steps: 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24 · 32. The grid is the rule; the corrections are the craft. Text sits 20 from a control's edge but an icon sits 16, because a glyph's bounding box carries its own air and centring it mathematically makes it look pushed out. Icon-to-label gap is 8 — deliberately tighter than the edge inset so the pair reads as one object.

4pt column grid · control insets snap to it

4 — Motion: physical, never decorative

Motion responds to how fast you moved, not to a fixed clock. Drags settle with the velocity they were released at; dismissals fire on distance or fling. This is felt rather than seen, and it is the thing a user remembers in the hand.

press in 120ms
settle / release 220ms · inherits release velocity
surface enter 320ms decelerate

Press feedback is a 0.97 scale, not a colour flash and never a ripple — ripple is Material's signature and we do not borrow it.
